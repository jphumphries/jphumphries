About me
In the past nearly 20 years, I’ve had the privilege of working for several companies with increasing tech integration. With that came the necessity for security measures to protect internal systems. For quite a while, these measures were things I knew existed but didn’t have a close understanding of the design, complexities, and necessary policies to manage them.
Having limited knowledge of the systems working behind the scenes, I found myself always questioning why certain systems worked as they did to allow associates to use appropriate software or access seemingly necessary data. It was clear to me that policy sometimes stood in the way of effective customer service.
For instance, during my time working for Amazon as a low tier associate it was a curious thing to me when certain people were given access to certain systems because of a certain level of training. I found myself wondering why others as well as myself had learned to do the same work but were not given access to the same systems. 
Two problems are highlighted here: First that associates were using the credentials of other officially trained associates, and not enough associates had been run through the proper channels to gain necessary access in a timely manner. These IAM systems were not perfect, and in retrospect, it was unclear as to why we were disallowed access to the appropriate systems and software. It was later revealed that only a certain number of associates were allowed to have access to those systems.
